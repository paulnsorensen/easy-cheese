# Empirical evidence on parallel sub-agent counts and fan-out thresholds

## Research question
Is there measured/empirical evidence on how many parallel sub-agents to spawn, and on when fan-out beats a single agent? Motivated by ~70 unmeasured fan-out constants in a local pipeline (n in {1,2,5}, waves capped at 4, "2-3 workers", "2-4 reviewers", worker pools 1-16).

## Direct answers

**1. Is there ANY published measurement that would let someone derive a defensible default for "how many sub-agents", or is the whole field guessing?**
Partial evidence exists, but nothing transfers cleanly to arbitrary constants like "2-3 workers" or "waves capped at 4". What's measured: (a) token-cost multipliers for multi-agent vs single-agent (Anthropic, `<certain>`), (b) diminishing/negative returns curves for *sample count* in self-consistency/majority-voting (multiple papers, `<certain>` for the specific benchmarks tested), (c) panel-size studies for LLM-judge ensembles converging on ~3 (`<certain>` for the cited benchmarks, but panel-size ≠ agent-fan-out-count — different task shape). None of these studies establish a universal "optimal N" — every number is benchmark- and task-specific, and none evaluate a coding-review-fan-out pipeline like the one in question. So: **evidence exists for the shape of the curve (diminishing/flattening returns, sometimes negative at high N), not for a transferable magic number.**

**2. Where do published numbers actually come from — benchmark, ablation, or vibes?**
Mixed, and it matters which claim you're citing:
- Anthropic's 15×/4× token multipliers and 90.2% performance delta: **internal eval / production telemetry**, not a published academic ablation — Anthropic states the methodology (BrowseComp regression analysis explaining 95% of variance) but the underlying eval set and full experimental design aren't public. `<certain>` that the numbers were measured; `<speculative>` on how well BrowseComp/their internal research eval generalizes to other task types.
- Self-consistency sample-count curves (arXiv 2511.00751): **benchmark ablation** — HotpotQA and MATH-500, Gemini 2.5, sweeping sample count. `<certain>`, but scoped to those two benchmarks/model family.
- Parallel scaling K=1→4 (arXiv 2602.18998, "Benchmark Test-Time Scaling of General LLM Agents"): **benchmark ablation**, capped at K=4 for API cost reasons — the paper itself doesn't test higher N, so it cannot speak to whether returns keep rising past 4. `<certain>` for K≤4 on their benchmark; `<don't know>` beyond that.
- PoLL / "Replacing Judges with Juries" panel-size-3 convergence: **benchmark ablation** on evaluator agreement tasks, not agent fan-out. `<certain>` for judge-panel-agreement tasks; `<speculative>` if generalized to reviewer-agent fan-out in a code pipeline (different task, different failure mode).
- Cognition's "Don't Build Multi-Agents" and "Multi-Agents: What's Actually Working": **asserted from production experience**, no benchmark numbers published. Explicitly a counterweight, not a measurement. `<certain>` that no numbers are given; the underlying claims are `<speculative>` (practitioner report, not ablation).
- Multi-agent debate round-count diminishing returns: cited by later papers as "previous work shows diminishing returns with more rounds" but the round-count-vs-accuracy curve itself is scattered across debate papers testing 2-5 rounds typically; treat as `<certain>` that diminishing returns are reported, `<speculative>` on exact round count where it flattens (varies by paper).

**3. What is the reported cost curve for parallel agents (token multiplier per agent)?**
Only Anthropic publishes a clean number: single agent ≈ **4× a plain chat conversation** in tokens; multi-agent (orchestrator + subagents) ≈ **15× a plain chat conversation**. This is a multiplier on the *whole system*, not "Nx per additional subagent" — Anthropic does not publish a per-subagent marginal-cost curve (e.g., cost at N=2 vs N=5 vs N=10 subagents). `<certain>` for the 4×/15× figures as stated; `<don't know>` on the marginal per-agent cost curve — not found in any source checked (Anthropic's post, the practitioner blogs summarizing it, or the benchmark papers found).

## Open questions (alternatives raised by sources, not recommendations)
- Anthropic's numbers are scoped to breadth-first research/browsing tasks (BrowseComp); they explicitly say coding is a worse fit for multi-agent fan-out due to shared-context and dependency requirements — this is directly relevant to a code-review pipeline and cuts against blanket adoption of their multipliers as justification for high fan-out in code contexts.
- PoLL's panel-size-3 finding is about judge/evaluator agreement, not agent task-completion fan-out — treating it as evidence for "3 reviewers" conflates two different measurement setups (annotator agreement vs. independent task completion). Flagged as an open question, not resolved here.
- The "Benchmark Test-Time Scaling" paper caps K at 4 purely for API cost reasons — it cannot be cited as evidence that returns flatten at N=4 specifically; it only shows gains through N=4 and is silent beyond that.
- No source found (in the sources checked: Anthropic's post, Cognition's two posts, Devin docs, the benchmark papers above, and targeted searches for Cursor/Sourcegraph/Factory/Shopify engineering blogs) that reports a specific "we tried N and settled on M because of measured X" for coding-agent worker-pool sizing. The Cursor/Sourcegraph/Factory practitioner-report searches in this pass returned no on-topic results (search noise only) — this is a gap, not a confirmed absence; a deeper crawl of those specific engineering blogs was not completed in this pass.
- Self-refine/reflexion iteration-count-vs-returns curves were located at the paper-existence level (Madaan et al. 2023, self-refine) but this pass did not extract the specific round-count-diminishing-returns numbers from the primary PDF — flagged as unresolved, not fabricated.

## Confidence
Overall: **moderate**. The Anthropic token-multiplier and performance-delta numbers are `<certain>` (primary source, directly quoted). The diminishing-returns shape for sample/panel/round counts is `<certain>` as a qualitative pattern across multiple independent benchmark papers, but the specific "optimal N" numbers are benchmark-specific and do not transfer to a code-review-fan-out pipeline without further ablation. The practitioner-report leg (Cursor, Sourcegraph, Factory, Shopify with explicit "we tried N, settled on M") came up empty in this search pass — treat that gap as "not found in the sources checked," not as "doesn't exist."
