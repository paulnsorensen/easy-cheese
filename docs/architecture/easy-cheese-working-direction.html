Three boundaries to keep clear

Evidence ≠ assertion

A schema-valid result or self-review is not proof that behavior works.

Memory ≠ authority

A linked old proposal is not an approved current requirement.

More process ≠ quality

Another reviewer, document, or gate must add useful assurance.

Open the right questions

When is a piece ready to build?

Which unknowns block implementation, which can remain open, and what must be stable before another worker starts?

What must the human understand and approve?

Ruled: Hard-cheese stays the quiz at publication. A separate review artifact carries understanding through the work; a follow-up mold designs it.

What happens when parallel work becomes stale?

Ruled: the in-flight piece finishes and a follow-up is queued. A change in direction cancels the piece and resends it.

What result earns a rewrite?

Ruled: make the ruled changes first and measure after; correct course if a change proves wrong. Journeys and the grader follow the tilth benchmark work.
